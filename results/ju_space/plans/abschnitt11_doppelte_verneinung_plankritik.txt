VERDICT:
Both parts of the plan aim at what the user asked about. As written, though, Part A would mostly measure two things this model already fixes: the mirror symmetry between Q2 and Q3, and the mean of the Q3 write. Part B1 would loop a direction that section 10 showed is not about RSI. One model load (about 8 minutes at 2 threads, the 902 positions of the 16 main texts plus one thinking trace) settles much of this before any run.

Q2 and Q3 are equally common in every block (Q3/(Q2+Q3) = 0.494-0.505) and equally harmful to remove. Zeroing Q3 in all blocks gives KL 2.92 and raises cross-entropy from 3.63 to 6.49; zeroing Q2 gives KL 2.86. Replacing Q3 by its per-unit mean leaves KL 0.43, so 85% of the damage is the mean. Replacing it by its mean at the live Q3 positions leaves KL 0.19. A random set matched to Q3 in count and |a| costs only 0.96, because its signs are mixed. Zeroing Q3 in blocks 0-9, 10-18 and 19-27 gives KL 1.41, 0.32 and 0.016. Keeping the top 50% by |a| costs KL 0.056 and keeps 41-52% of the Q3 entries (78-94% of their |a|-mass). Restoring the dropped Q3 or the dropped Q2 entries halves that KL equally (0.027 and 0.029).

What follows from this:
- 'Q3 zeroed in all blocks' leaves no reasoning to sustain.
- The planned sparsity grid of 2-20% is the regime where section 6.1 found the model broken.
- Every Q3 claim needs the same test with Q2, on a basis that preserves the mean.

A4 has further problems. It uses greedy decoding, which Qwen's model card explicitly advises against in thinking mode (endless repetition). Its 512-token budget is one the baseline will often hit: the check trace had only reached the second of four steps of a simple problem after 256 tokens. With N = 8 binary outcomes, only extreme effects can be seen. The slide itself also argues against the plan's reading that timing matters: time-reversed activations and another problem's activations largely work. What fails is one contiguous block, statistically matched surrogates and a constant.

Recommendation, by part:
- Text gate, about 30 minutes. Q3 against Q2, mean-preserving, per block group; fix the sparsity level k* in advance.
- A4, rebuilt. Primary measures are teacher-forced along sampled long traces: KL by position bin, and the pressure to emit '</think>'. Bases are interventions that leave short text nearly intact: Q3 zeroed in blocks 19-27, Q3 at its conditional mean, top-k at k*. The slide's decisive contrasts are included. Free generation only confirms, for 3-4 conditions.
- B1. Keep it with fixes: start directions on text tokens (the nesting step of 10.5 for 'improves' against 'describes'), a noise ceiling, a step size calibrated per direction, batched generation. State plainly that it can only show that the model reads its steered text back as the same direction. Nothing learns.
- B2. Drop as specified. Its outcome is set by generation 0's accuracy, its test problems get pseudo-labels, and it costs about 10 times the plan's estimate. An optional version comparing a chain with one direct step can follow if the user wants the learning reading of Q2. Neither version has a meta-level.

Budget: about 3 hours for the unconditional stages, about 5.5 hours with the conditional ones, plus about 2.5 hours on all 4 cores for the optional toy runs.

[P02] (high) Q2 and Q3 are mirror halves in this model (algebra and measurement)
  PROBLEM: For g < 0, a = silu(g)*u with silu(g) between -0.28 and 0. So Q2 and Q3 are the u > 0 and u < 0 halves of one small, nearly linear leak. Because g and u are nearly uncorrelated (9.4: median 0.06-0.19), the two halves must be equally common and equally large. Measured: Q3/(Q2+Q3) = 0.494-0.505 in all 28 blocks. Top-k survival of Q2 and Q3 agrees within 0.007 at every k. The Q3/Q2 write RMS ratio is 0.94-1.07. Zeroing gives KL 2.92 (Q3) vs 2.86 (Q2); restoring after top-50% gives 0.027 vs 0.029. On the one trace, zeroing Q2 raised the mean probability of '</think>' far more than zeroing Q3 (3.3e-3 vs 1.1e-5; baseline 9e-11). A Q3 result without the identical Q2 result cannot be called 'double-negative'.
  FIX: Run every Q3 condition also with Q2 (same blocks, same replacement). Call an effect Q3-specific only if it is at least twice the Q2 effect on a mean-preserving basis (rules A-gate, A4a, A4c). If Q2 does the same, report it as an effect of the negative-gate leak (the mechanism of README 7.4), not of the double negative.
[P03] (high) Zeroing Q3 mostly moves the mean of the MLP write
  PROBLEM: Q3 activations are all positive, so zeroing them shifts the MLP write by -sum_i E[a_i*1(Q3)]*w_i at every position. Per block, 26-76% of the energy of the Q3 write is its constant mean over positions. On the 902 positions:
- Q3 -> per-unit mean: KL 0.43 instead of 2.92 (85% of the damage is the mean).
- Q3 -> its per-unit mean at the live Q3 positions: KL 0.19.
- Matched random set (same count, same |a| bins, drawn from non-Q3 entries, 99.7% matched): KL 0.96.
The matched set costs less only because its signs are mixed, so its mean shift partly cancels. 'Q3 matters more than random activations of the same size' would be an artifact of sign coherence.
  FIX: Use mean-preserving replacements as the bases for every question about the information in Q3: Q3 -> per-unit mean (at every position) and Q3 -> conditional mean at live Q3 positions. Take the means from pool texts, not from the text being evaluated. Draw matched random sets within the same sign and mean-preserve them too. Report zeroing only as a descriptive row.
[P04] (high) Zeroing Q3 is not a usable base for reasoning tests
