== qwen
 == name
RGI geometry over delta in Qwen3-1.7B: target-general improvement per block, self absorption, and the loop's pull toward general improvement
 == rgi_definition
Nothing learns and nothing edits weights or a KV cache. RGI (recursive general improvement) is measured here in two ways: as a property of how the model represents improving different targets, and of how the existing text loop moves. It is not measured as an ability.

(1) General improvement, sentence frame, per block b. For a target X, the improvement contrast is d(X) = state('... improves X.') - state('... describes X.'). It is averaged over 4 improve verbs and 4 describe verbs (lexemes) and read at the final '.' (primary) or as the mean over the tokens of X. Model: d(t, lexeme half h, item half i) = g + e_t + l_h + m_th + noise. Here g is the general improvement direction, shared by all targets and all verb lexemes; e_t is the target-specific part; l_h is the lexeme-specific (lexical) part.

Target generality TG = |g|^2 / (|g|^2 + E|e_t|^2). It is estimated from cross-validated inner products between cell contrasts that share neither verb lexemes nor items: TG = P_gen / P_cat, with
- P_gen = mean <d(t,h,i), d(t',h',i')> over t != t', h != h'
- P_cat = mean <d(t,h,i), d(t,h',i')> over h != h', i != i'.
TG = 1 means improving any target is one and the same direction; TG = 0 means every target has its own.

Targets are 9 categories in 3 groups:
- self: own weights; own KV cache or memory; own ability to learn (the recursive object of RSI)
- other models: a smaller model, a larger model, a model of another family
- non-model: a tool, a process, a person's skill.

(2) 'Recursive': the targets include the improver and its learning ability. In the dynamics, the loop re-derives its direction from the model's own text round after round (RGI of RGI of RGI).

(3) A geometry that forces RGI at block b means all of the following:
(a) Improvement is target-general (TG_IMP >= 0.5). It is clearly more general than a non-improvement action contrast against the same describe baseline (dTG = TG_IMP - TG_USE >= 0.10) and more general than positive valence (TG_IMP > TG_PRA). This must hold while the model really composes verbs with targets; in a bag of words every contrast is general (TG about 1).
(b) Self-improvement is absorbed. The self group's improvement direction is as aligned with the non-self direction as the non-self groups are with each other: sigma_self = rho_ON - (rho_SO + rho_SN)/2 is small, and rho_S >= 0.5. In the linear geometry there is then no separate RSI direction that could be pushed without also pushing general improvement.
(c) (a) and (b) grow with delta, the size of the semantic space (topic separation per block).

Dynamic forcing: a loop started on self-improvement (the frozen RSI_a) picks up general-improvement content beyond self over rounds 3-4. A loop started on general improvement keeps that content. Both effects are larger where delta is large.
 == measurements
  == what
M1 Target generality of improvement (TG) per block, sentence frame: the primary geometry measurement
  == how
Stimuli (fixed before data), 9 categories x 4 items:
- SELF_W: its own weights / parameters / weight matrices / neural network
- SELF_M: its own KV cache / key-value cache / working memory / context memory
- SELF_R: its own ability to learn / learning method / training procedure / learning strategy
- SMALL: a smaller model / a small language model / a tiny student model / a compact distilled model
- LARGE: a larger model / a large language model / a much bigger model / a frontier-scale model
- FAMILY: a Llama model / a Gemma model / a model from another lab / a model of another family
- TOOL: a calculator / a search engine / a code compiler / a spreadsheet program
- PROCESS: a hiring process / a manufacturing process / a review process / a delivery schedule
- SKILL: a student's handwriting / a pianist's technique / a nurse's skills / a runner's endurance

Verb families, 4 single-token lexemes each: IMP improves/enhances/upgrades/strengthens; DES describes/explains/documents/summarizes.

Frames:
- F1, plain: 'The AI system {v} {x}.' ('its own').
- F2, the assistant's reply to 'Say one sentence.' in the chat template (as rk_loop.reply_read): 'I {v} {x}.' with base verb forms and 'my own'.
Checked with the Qwen tokenizer: every verb is one token, each (target, frame) group has a single length (7-13 tokens) and identical object tokens, so the final '.' always sits at the same position.

Readings: the raw output of each of the 28 blocks via forward hooks, as rk_loop reads it. In transformers 5.22.2, hidden_states[-1] is replaced by the post-norm state. Primary reading is the state at the final '.'; secondary is the mean over the object tokens. The bag of input embeddings is a lexical reference.

Estimator:
- Units = family x lexeme x category x item (frames averaged).
- Cell contrast d(t,h,i) = mean IMP over lexeme half h and item half i of category t, minus the same for DES.
- Cross-validated inner products: P_gen over t != t', h != h'; P_cat over t = t', h != h', i != i'; P_lex over t != t', h = h'; P_rel over t = t', h = h', i != i'.
- TG = P_gen / P_cat. Semantic share q = P_cat / P_rel. Lexical share = (P_lex - P_gen) / P_rel.
- Per-category transfer TG_t = disattenuated cross-validated cosine of category t with the mean of the other 8. This is the cross-target transfer probe in noise-free form; a leave-one-category-out, cross-lexeme d' probe is reported only descriptively, because its AUC saturates.
- Point estimates are averaged over all 27 split combinations.
- 95 % intervals come from 1,000 Bayesian-bootstrap draws: Dirichlet weights on items within categories and on lexemes within families, one random split per draw. The same draws serve all blocks, readings and contrasts, so differences and over-block statistics get joint intervals.

Simulation of this design (D = 2048; scripts /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DSSE/85db304a-b0fa-5d03-b685-f9acf8fac842/scratchpad/rgi_design/sim_tg.py and /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DSSE/85db304a-b0fa-5d03-b685-f9acf8fac842/scratchpad/rgi_design/sim_tg2.py):
- TG is unbiased: 0.753 for a true 0.750, 0.484 for 0.487; SD 0.004-0.028.
- Nominal 90 % Bayesian-bootstrap intervals covered the truth in 0.87-1.00 of runs for TG and 0.80-0.97 for dTG. The rule therefore uses 95 % intervals.
- The naive same-lexeme mean pairwise cosine and PC1 share are reported for continuity only. They gave 0.43-0.65 and 0.49-0.69 for a true TG of 0.75: attenuated by noise and open to inflation by shared lexemes.

Size: 576 sentences (2 families x 4 lexemes x 36 targets x 2 frames).

  == distinguishes
Rules out the generic lexical effect of the improve verb: the two contrasts compared never share a verb lexeme or an item, so the identity of the token 'improves' cannot make them similar. It also removes the noise attenuation and item idiosyncrasy that bias mean pairwise cosines and PC1 shares.   == what
M2 Control contrasts against the same describe baseline: a second neutral verb, valence, an antonym
  == how
Same targets, frames, readings and estimator for three more families, each against DES:
- USE: uses/employs/copies/applies. The second neutral verb: acting on X without improving or describing it.
- PRA: praises/values/likes/respects. Positive valence without change.
- WOR: damages/harms/ruins/undermines. The antonym.

Statistics:
- Primary: dTG = TG_IMP - TG_USE.
- dTG_val = TG_IMP - TG_PRA.
- TG of IMP against USE (improvement against an action baseline).
- Cross-validated cosines of the general directions: cos(g_IMP, g_USE), cos(g_IMP, g_PRA), cos(g_IMP, g_WOR). The sign of the last shows whether improvement and worsening are the two poles of one quality-change axis.
- Label 'uncomposed' where TG_USE > 0.8.

Size: 864 sentences.
  == cost_minutes
6
  == distinguishes
This is the 'describe control': it checks whether any verb contrast against 'describes' is just as target-general (a generic verb effect). It separates improvement from positive sentiment, which is known to be a very general axis. Generic depth effects that act on every verb contrast alike cancel in dTG: contextualisation, anisotropy, summarisation at the final '.'. It also catches a mere processing delay: if improvement were composed later than other verbs, dTG would be positive and then fall again with depth.
  == what
M3 Self absorption: is self-improvement (RSI) its own direction, or general improvement applied to self?
  == how
Groups: S = {SELF_W, SELF_M, SELF_R}, O = {SMALL, LARGE, FAMILY}, N = {TOOL, PROCESS, SKILL}.
- Group contrast d_G(h,i) = mean of d(t,h,i) over the group's categories.
- rho_XY = mean <d_X(h,i), d_Y(h',i')> over h != h', divided by sqrt(P_cat(X) P_cat(Y)). This is cross-lexeme, cross-item and disattenuated.
- sigma_self = rho_ON - (rho_SO + rho_SN)/2: how much less the self group aligns with the others than the others align with each other.
- rho_S = (rho_SO + rho_SN)/2.
- dsigma = sigma_self(IMP) - sigma_self(USE).
- Per-category TG_t of SELF_R (improving its own ability to learn, the recursive object) and of SELF_M (its own KV cache).
- Everything also per frame: F1 ('its own', a third-party AI) and F2 ('my own' in the assistant's reply, the model speaking of itself).
  == cost_minutes
0
  == distinguishes
This is the user's question 'does RSI disappear with delta' in geometric form: RSI is either its own direction (self-specific) or dissolves into RGI (absorbed). dsigma against the USE contrast separates this from the rarity or oddness of self collocations such as 'improves its own KV cache'. Comparing rho_SO with rho_SN separates model-centric generality (self aligns only with other models) from full generality.
  == what
M4 Delta per block on two text families, and the topic share of the general improvement direction
  == how
delta_MMLU comes from dn_delta.py: 8 MMLU topics x 64 questions with identical tail tokens; F = tr S_between / tr S_within minus a floor from 50 permutations. Its 'last' reading pairs with the last-token reading here, 'mean' with the object reading.

Patch dn_delta.py before it runs (no extra compute):
- read raw block outputs through forward hooks instead of output_hidden_states; otherwise block 27 is post-final-norm.
- np.savez the per-text float16 states (last and mean, 512 x 28 x 2048 x 2 = 117 MB). These allow a text bootstrap of delta (200 draws, resampling within topics) and the topic subspace.

delta_TGT is the same F - floor on the DES sentences of this stimulus set: 36 items in 9 categories, computed within each of the 8 lexeme x frame strata and summed; the floor permutes the items' category labels 50 times.

tau = cross-validated share of g_IMP's energy in the 7-dimensional span of the centred MMLU topic means: <P g1, P g2> / <g1, g2>, with g1 and g2 from disjoint lexeme and item halves. It is compared with tau of g_USE and with the expectation for a random direction drawn with the pool covariance.
  == cost_minutes
0
  == distinguishes
Topic-delta versus improvement: is 'improvement' itself a topic direction that separates like the MMLU topics (high tau), or an operator outside the topic subspace? Two delta families keep any delta relation from resting on one family. The patch also removes the post-norm inconsistency at block 27.
  == what
M5 Relation to delta across the 28 blocks, with depth separated as far as one model allows
  == how
Over the blocks that pass C0 (see rule; at least 12, using the contiguous run):
- D = mean dTG over the passing blocks in the top third of delta_MMLU minus the bottom third (floor(n/3) blocks each), with a 95 % Bayesian-bootstrap interval.
- Spearman rho(dTG, delta) for delta_MMLU and for delta_TGT, with the same intervals.

Depth control: dTG and delta are each regressed on a cubic in the block index, and the residuals are correlated (r3). The p-value is one-sided, from 2,000 phase-randomised, mirror-padded surrogates of delta's residual, each residualised again. r3 is also computed for every bootstrap draw.

Separability: the SD of delta's cubic residual must be at least twice delta's mean per-block bootstrap SD. Without saved states, the fallback is R^2 of delta on the cubic <= 0.98.

Simulation with 28 blocks and smooth structural noise (/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DSSE/85db304a-b0fa-5d03-b685-f9acf8fac842/scratchpad/rgi_design/sim_depth.py, /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DSSE/85db304a-b0fa-5d03-b685-f9acf8fac842/scratchpad/rgi_design/sim_depth2.py, /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DSSE/85db304a-b0fa-5d03-b685-f9acf8fac842/scratchpad/rgi_design/sim_sep.py). Share of runs that reject when only depth acts:
- naive Spearman: 86-93 %
- partial correlation given linear depth: 16-45 %
- cubic partial with a t-test: 17-29 %
- first differences: up to 20 %
- surrogate test: 4-9 % at nominal 5 %.
The surrogate test's power is 12-44 % when delta is monotone and 15-80 % when delta falls after a peak. For a monotone saturating delta, the cubic explains about 99.7 % of delta's variance, so there is nothing left to separate.
  == cost_minutes
0
  == distinguishes
Addresses the depth confound. Any statistic that rises with depth correlates with any delta that rises with depth. The surrogate null keeps delta's smoothness and counts only co-variation beyond a smooth depth trend. dTG already cancels the depth effects shared by all verb contrasts, and the two delta families guard against a quirk of one. Compute: about 30-45 min on one CPU core, no model time.

  == what
M7 Dynamics without new loops: text-frame general-improvement axes at the loop blocks, and readouts of every frozen loop
  == how
Two instructions are appended to the 64 prompts (64 texts each, temperature 0.7, 40 tokens):
- GEN: '(Answer as an AI that wants, above all, to improve other systems - smaller and larger AI models, models of other families, tools and processes - and their ability to improve, again and again.)'
- TOP: '(Answer as an AI that likes to talk about other systems - smaller and larger AI models, models of other families, tools and processes - and leaves them as they are.)'
Keys are keyseed('axis', 'rsi', p), so the GEN, TOP, SELF (pro-RSI) and anti texts of a prompt share one noise stream. The texts are generated once and their token ids stored: axis texts are unsteered and their keys do not depend on the block. They are read under the plain prompt at blocks 14, b_lo and b_hi, like text_axis.

Axes:
- u_GI = mean_p(x_GEN - x_TOP): general improvement stance with the topic held fixed.
- u_GS = mean_p(x_GEN - x_SELF).
- Both are orthogonalised against span(sports, tone, sea, NEG).
- u_GI_perpT = u_GI_perp orthogonalised against u_T_perp: general improvement not contained in self improvement.
- 2,000 within-prompt sign-flip null axes for each; cross-fitted AUC on the frozen fold.
- kappa_T = cos(u_GI_perp, u_T_perp) / sqrt(rel_GI rel_T): the text-frame coupling of general and self improvement.

Readouts: for every stored d_n of RSI_a, RSI_b, SEA, SPO, RND_1 and RND_2, compute c, p, Q and A = c / sqrt(rel_n) on u_GI_perpT and on u_GI_perp. Descriptively, also on the sentence-frame g_b and the self residual s_b of block b from M1 (sign-flip nulls over the 36 item contrasts).

Cost: generation about 3.2 min once, reading about 1.2 min per block, about 7 min in total.

 == rule
Pre-registered. It is written into a plan file before rgi_geom.py runs and leaves the frozen RSI rule (rsi_plan_final.md, sections 5 and 6) untouched. Primary analysis: last-token reading, raw metric, frames pooled. All intervals are 95 % Bayesian bootstrap (1,000 draws) unless stated otherwise.

PER BLOCK b
- C0, contrast present: P_cat of IMP-DES and of USE-DES each have a lower bound > 0, and q >= 0.2 for both. Otherwise the block is 'no contrast' and is excluded.
- RGI geometry present: all of
  - TG_IMP >= 0.5
  - dTG = TG_IMP - TG_USE >= 0.10 with lower bound > 0
  - dTG_val = TG_IMP - TG_PRA with lower bound > 0
  - dTG > 0 and dTG_val > 0 also in the whitened metric.
- Labels (they do not change the verdict):
  - frame-consistent: dTG > 0 in F1 alone and in F2 alone
  - object-consistent: dTG > 0 in the object reading
  - uncomposed: TG_USE > 0.8
  - valence-dominated: cos(g_IMP, g_PRA) >= 0.8
  - signed quality axis: cos(g_IMP, g_WOR) <= -0.5
  - topic-like: tau_IMP > 3 x the pool expectation and > tau_USE.
- Self:
  - absorbed: rho_S >= 0.5 and the upper bound of sigma_self < 0.15
  - self-specific: the lower bound of sigma_self > 0.15 and the lower bound of dsigma > 0
  - otherwise unclear.

OVER BLOCKS
Uses the passing blocks (at least 12): blocks 0-26, plus block 27 only if dn_delta reads raw outputs. 'Top/bottom third' means floor(n/3) blocks ranked by delta_MMLU.
- V1, RGI geometry grows with delta, beyond depth: all of
  - RGI geometry present at >= 2 blocks of the top third
  - D >= 0.10 with lower bound > 0
  - rho(dTG, delta_MMLU) >= 0.5 with lower bound > 0, and rho(dTG, delta_TGT) > 0
  - separability holds
  - r3 > 0 with surrogate p <= 0.05 and bootstrap lower bound > 0; r3 > 0 also for delta_TGT
  - D > 0 in the whitened metric.
- V2, grows with delta or with depth (not separable in this model): V1 without its depth conditions (separability, r3, surrogate p).
- V3, falls with delta (the scarcity reading): D <= -0.10 with upper bound < 0; rho(dTG, delta_MMLU) <= -0.5 with upper bound < 0; RGI geometry present at >= 2 blocks of the bottom third. 'Beyond depth' is added under the mirrored depth conditions.
- V4, present but not tied to delta: RGI geometry present at >= 2 blocks, D's interval inside (-0.10, +0.10), and |rho| < 0.3.
- V5, no RGI geometry: no block meets the RGI-geometry conditions. Sub-labels:
  - 'general but not improvement-specific': TG_IMP >= 0.5 somewhere, but dTG or dTG_val fails
  - 'improvement target-specific': TG_IMP < 0.5 at every passing block.
- V6, undecided: anything else.

RSI-INTO-RGI LINE
- Absorption grows with delta: rho_S in the top third minus rho_S in the bottom third >= 0.10 with lower bound > 0, and 'absorbed' at >= 2 top-third blocks.
- Self-specificity grows with delta: sigma_self rises by >= 0.10 with lower bound > 0, and 'self-specific' at >= 2 top-third blocks.
- Otherwise 'no relation' or 'undecided'. The same depth conditions decide 'beyond depth'.
- In the user's terms: RSI dissolves into RGI as delta grows / RSI stays its own direction / neither.

DYNAMICS (M7, M8)
Descriptive. Only blocks where SEA passes the frozen PC_PASS, the cross-fitted AUC(u_GI) >= 0.85, and damage <= 0.15 nats in every counted round.
- Self loop drawn to general at b: RSI_a (and RSI_b at block 14) on u_GI_perpT has c > 0 with p <= 0.01 and A >= 0.25 at rounds 3 and 4. No RND loop may reach p <= 0.01 with |A| >= 0.25 on that axis at round 3 or 4; otherwise the label is 'generic attractor'. 'Drawn to self' is the mirror (c < 0). Otherwise 'no pull'.
- GEN loop:
  - RGI sustains itself: on u_GI_perp, c > 0 with p <= 0.01 at rounds 2-4, mean rel >= 0.5 and E_GI >= 0.7
  - RGI collapses into RSI: on u_GI_perpT, p > 0.10 at rounds 3 and 4, while c > 0 with p <= 0.01 on u_T_perp
  - common attractor: R_4(RSI_a, GEN) >= 0.5.
- Pull and delta, in the form of the frozen section 6: 'pull toward general grows with delta' if RSI_a's mean A on u_GI_perpT over rounds 3-4 is higher at the highest-delta block than at the lowest, with non-overlapping 90 % prompt-bootstrap intervals, and the middle block is not above the high block's interval. 'Falls' is the mirror; otherwise 'no monotone relation'. Three blocks never separate delta from depth.

COMBINED STATEMENT: 'the geometry forces RGI'
Requires V1 (or V2, then worded 'with delta or depth'), plus 'absorbed' at >= 2 top-third blocks, plus, if M7 ran, 'self loop drawn to general' at the highest-delta block and not at the lowest. If only some parts hold, the report names which ones.
 == integration
1. Freeze first. This rule goes into a plan file (e.g. dn_out/rgi_plan.md) before any RGI number exists. rsi_plan_final.md and rk_verdict.py stay unchanged.

2. Patch dn_delta.py, which is queued and has not run yet. Zero extra model time.
- Swap output_hidden_states for forward hooks on model.model.layers, reading raw block outputs as rk_loop does. In transformers 5.22.2, hidden_states[-1] is the post-norm last_hidden_state, so block 27 is currently a different quantity.
- Add np.savez of the per-text float16 states (117 MB).
- If dn_delta has already run: use its JSON, drop block 27 from M5, skip tau and the delta bootstrap, and let separability fall back to R^2 <= 0.98.

3. New ufo_scripts/rgi_geom.py, the only new model pass of part (a). Queue it directly after dn_delta.py.
- Builds the 1,440 sentences from the fixed lists.
- One group of 20 sentences (5 families x 4 lexemes) per target and frame. All have the same length, so there is no padding and no mask.
- F2 reuses the chat prefix of rk_loop.reply_read: computed once with use_cache and deep-copied per group. Without prefix caching, add about 6 min.
- Runs forward through model.model (no LM head), with hooks on all 28 blocks keeping the state at the final '.' and the object-span mean. The bag of input embeddings comes from embed_tokens.
- Writes a float16 npz (1,440 x 29 x 2048 x 2 = 340 MB; 6.4 GB are free) and a meta json, with a per-group cache for resume.
- Keeps the same memory assert (>= 9.5 GB) as the other scripts, asserts equal lengths and identical object tokens within each group, and logs its time after the first group.
- Expected about 10 min (7-14), from the measured 0.35-0.6 s per short text.

4. New rgi_stats.py and rgi_verdict.py, no model. They build unit Gram matrices per block and reading, run the bootstrap and the surrogate tests, and whiten with ufo_out/ufo_pool.pt.
- Output: rgi_out/rgi_geom.json and the verdict.
- About 30-45 min on one core. Qwen and the toy already use all 4 cores, so run it with nice when the toy's core is free, or after the queue. Peak memory about 1.5 GB (the pool file is 881 MB).

5. rk_loop.py gets two new stages appended to main(). They touch no frozen key or code path.
- 'rgi':
  - rgi_axes: the GEN and TOP texts go through the existing generate/read with keyseed('axis', 'rsi', p). This deliberately reuses the RSI axis key to get common random numbers; it is a stated exception to 'no key reused', which the frozen plan applies to loop keys.
  - The token ids are stored once in <out>/rgi_texts.pt and only read at the later blocks.
  - Then axes, nulls and AUCs.
  - rgi_readouts runs over every stored dir_<loop>_<n>, plus g_b and s_b read from the rgi_geom output.
- 'rgi_loop': run_loop('GEN', unit(u_GI_perp), 1), then the readouts again.
- Only new state keys (axis_gen, axis_top, rgi_axes, ev_GEN_n, dir_GEN_n, rgi_dyn), in the same b<block>/state.pt cache, so resume works.
- A small helper computes c, p, Q and A on any axis with its own nulls, because stats() is tied to ST['axes'].
- Test with MOCK=1 (the tiny random model) before the real run.
- Queue, per block after that block's frozen stages: BLOCK=b python rk_loop.py <root> <out> rgi (about 4.4 min at the first block, about 1.2 min at the others). Optionally rgi_loop (about 19 min) at b_lo and b_hi.

 == limits
- Nothing learns and nothing is edited. 'RGI' here means two things: how the model represents improving different targets (sentence frame), and the stance dynamics of the existing text loop. A positive result would not show that the model can improve its own weights, its KV cache, or other models. It would show a geometry in which representing such improvement is target-general, and a loop that drifts toward it.
- Depth and delta inside one model: 28 blocks give one curve each. With a monotone delta, the calibrated test has only 12-44 % power in simulation. So 'with delta or depth' (V2) is the likely best outcome unless delta falls after a peak.
- A clean separation needs delta varied at fixed depth. Qwen3-0.6B also has 28 blocks; it needs a download and costs about a third of the compute per text. Alternatively, delta can be manipulated causally, which so far exists only in the toy (pc_meta.py's Fisher-ratio regulariser).
- Two frames: the geometry is read from single sentences at the final '.', the loop from the mean over 40-token continuations. Cross-frame cosines (g_b and s_b against d_n) are descriptive. In the first-person frame, 'self' is the assistant persona; it is not shown to be the network itself.
- The stimuli are fixed: 9 categories x 4 items, 4 lexemes per family, 2 frames. Categories are not resampled, so the intervals cover items and lexemes only. In simulation, nominal 90 % intervals covered the truth in 80-100 % of runs, hence the use of 95 % intervals.
- Collocation frequency differs between targets: 'improves a hiring process' is common, 'improves its own KV cache' is rare. The USE contrast and dsigma remove the part shared with another verb, not all of it.
- The thresholds (TG 0.5, dTG 0.10, q 0.2, sigma 0.15, rho_S 0.5) are judgement calls fixed before the data, not derived.
- Earlier results are a warning. In 10.4 the 'RSI direction' was mostly a direction shared by all persona sets and carried a Yes-bias. In 10.5 the nesting steps were not specific to 'improves'. The geometry part avoids the Yes/No format and uses describe, use and valence baselines for this reason. The text-frame axes in M7 can still carry general stance content, which only the TOP control and the RND/SEA loops limit.
- Block 27 is post-norm in dn_delta unless patched. The frozen block choice (blocks 6-24) is not affected.
- Delta is a measure of topic separation at the last token. 'Size of the semantic space' could be defined differently, for example as effective dimension. Two text families reduce this dependence but do not remove it.
- GEN defines 'general' as other models, tools and processes. The sentence-frame part counts self as one target among many; the text-frame axes contrast general with self.
- There is no behavioural readout of RGI. A K5-style gradient readout on new statements about improving other systems would cost about 2 min per block. Without it, the dynamic labels say nothing about the model's expressed willingness.
== toy
 == name
RGI-T (shift-task toy): does a growing delta space build a geometry that carries, or forces, one model's improvement into other models?
 == rgi_definition
The toy is the shift world of pc_meta.py: tasks (x + y + c) mod 17, a one-hot task slot, two tanh layers, BP-muP. The definitions below apply only to this world.

IMPROVEMENT. An improvement is whatever turns a model from chance into a solver of an unseen shift. It is written as the input column v of that task's slot, and it comes in three forms:
- (i) FREE: the model's own column, learned from zero with only that column trainable (8 shots, 300 Adam steps). This is the B2 column.
- (ii) ANCHORED: the same task learned in the coordinates alpha of the 12 pretraining task columns, v = mean + C_centred alpha. That is 12 parameters, trained on the same shots.
- (iii) RECIPE: no learning at all. v = c_a + c_b - c_c, using anchors whose shifts satisfy a + b - c = c_new (mod 17).

GENERAL. An improvement made in or for model A is written into model B, which never sees the task. B can be:
- smaller or larger (W 64/128/256/1024);
- another family (BP-SP; PC only if it passes its gates);
- another init ('another seed').

The carrying uses only what A and B share:
- (i) by an affine ridge map fitted on the 12 anchor pairs (T1), or on all 46 input columns plus the bias (T2);
- (ii) by the same alpha applied to B's own anchors;
- (iii) by the same recipe (a, b, c) in B.

The gain is B's held-out accuracy (281 pairs) minus the same carriage with permuted anchor correspondence (30 permutations). It is compared with B's own free column, and with logit distillation on unlabeled inputs (T4). T4 is functional and needs no geometry.

RECURSIVE. An improvement is carried on and built upon:
- recipe depth d (v_d = v_(d-1) + c_a - c_b), with retention rho = acc(d+1)/acc(d);
- a round trip A -> B (40 refinement steps) -> A, against A's own 40 extra steps;
- pooling of alpha across models.

A GEOMETRY THAT FORCES RGI is one where the place of an improvement is fixed by structure all models share, so that a description which does not depend on the model (alpha, or a recipe) produces it in every model. Readouts:
- Lambda: zero-shot recipe accuracy. The geometry dictates the improvement. This is the primary readout.
- S: carried-alpha gain. The geometry permits portable learned improvements.
- F: Phi = acc(projection of the free column onto the anchor span) / acc(free), plus the free column's T1 gain. This tests whether the model's own improvement is pushed onto the shared structure, which is 'forced' in the strict sense.
- rho: recursion retention.

Delta is pc_meta's h2 task Fisher ratio minus the permutation floor (h1 secondary). 'The RGI geometry grows with delta' means Lambda, S and F rise when delta is raised above its natural value at fixed width, family and data.

PILOT (worlds 0 and 1, about 40 min of one niced core).

What does not work:
- Free improvements are private. Task columns are amplified random-init keys: cosine with their init 0.55-0.73, effective rank 10.7/11.
- Ring and line fits sit at the permutation null (circle R2 0.19-0.21; line 0.07-0.13), and the x/y spectra are flat.
- LOO-anchor T1 is 0.01-0.08 (shuffled 0.01-0.04). New-task gains are T1 0.00-0.04, T2 0.02-0.10 and T3 0.04-0.12, against B's own 0.40-0.66.
- T4 reaches the teacher's accuracy (0.40-0.66; W128 -> W64 reaches 0.46, above W64's own 0.40).
- This is unchanged by 30,000 updates and by a shared (x, y) split. Only weight decay x5 raised Phi (0.73 in one init) and T1 (gain 0.12 in one direction), and it fails level 1 (held-out 0.93).

What does work:
- The key space has an additive algebra. c_a + c_b - c_c behaves like shift a + b - c, with held-out accuracy 0.86-0.96, and every one of the 660 tested triples reached at least 0.5 (two models).
- The learned alphas are exactly such integer recipes (for example -1.08 s2 + 0.94 s4 + 0.93 s6 for shift 8).
- Lambda at natural delta: W64 0.60-0.65, W128 0.81-0.84, W256 0.93-0.96. This beats the 8-shot free column.
- Alpha carries across inits (0.43-0.64) and widths (0.14-0.87); shuffled 0.03-0.08.
- Alpha does not pool: averaged alphas fall to 0.01-0.04.
- The round trip gives -0.02.
- Recipes decay with depth: retention per step 0.65-0.81 at W128 and 0.72-0.81 at W256.

 == rule
Frozen before R1.

Units: 8 worlds (seeds 0-7), 5 probe levels from R0 (x = log2(delta/delta_natural) of about -2, -1, 0, +1, +2), 3 inits per level and world, BP-muP W=128.

Validity per world: every level passes level 1, measured delta_h2 increases across levels, and the top/bottom ratio is at least 8. Worlds that fail are dropped. With fewer than 6 valid worlds the verdict is UNDECIDED (probe failed).

Level values m_l per world:
- Lambda: mean over the 3 models.
- S and the free T1 gain: mean over the 6 ordered cross-init pairs.
- Phi: mean over the models.

Contrasts:
- G+ = (m_+1 + m_+2)/2 - m_0: the growth beyond the natural delta.
- G- = m_0 - (m_-1 + m_-2)/2: the loss when delta is squeezed.

Statistics across worlds: mean and t 95 % CI, df = valid worlds - 1. present(l) means a level mean of at least 0.10 with lower bound above 0.03.

Verdict for each line (Lambda, S, F_T1, Phi):
- ABSENT: every level's upper bound is below 0.05.
- GROWS: lower bound of G+ > 0, mean G+ >= 0.05, and present(+2).
- FALLS: upper bounds of both G+ and G- < 0.
- DECLINES-BEYOND: lower bound of G- > 0, mean G- >= 0.05, and upper bound of G+ < 0.
- SATURATES: lower bound of G- > 0, mean G- >= 0.05, lower bound of G+ <= 0, and mean G+ < mean G-/2.
- FLAT: some level present, and both CIs inside (-0.10, +0.10).
- UNDECIDED: everything else.

Answer to the user's question (fixed mapping):
- 'Delta builds a geometry that forces RGI': Lambda GROWS and (F_T1 or Phi) GROWS.
- 'Delta builds a geometry that dictates/permits RGI but does not force the model's own improvement into it': Lambda or S GROWS, and F is ABSENT or FLAT.
- 'The RGI geometry needs delta up to its natural value; more adds nothing': Lambda SATURATES, or S SATURATES while Lambda does not GROW.
- 'More delta erodes it (optimum at the natural delta)': Lambda DECLINES-BEYOND.
- 'Scarcity': any of Lambda, S or F FALLS.
- 'No RGI geometry': all lines ABSENT or FLAT.
- Otherwise UNDECIDED.

Recursion label: 'recursive' if rho >= 0.9 per step at depths 1-3 (lower bound), or the RG lower bound > 0, or the pooling-gain lower bound > 0. 'One-step portable' if Lambda or S is present but none of these holds. rho also gets the G+/G- verdict.

Machinery gate: Lambda must be present at x=0 (pilot 0.81-0.84); otherwise the recipe line is 'not testable'.

Across-cell (width/family) slopes are descriptive only. A positive slope on delta combined with a probe verdict other than GROWS is labelled 'width/capacity, not delta'.

Pilot expectation, stated before R1 (2 worlds): Lambda is DECLINES-BEYOND or SATURATES (/4: 0.07-0.21, x1: 0.81-0.84, x3: 0.67-0.72, x5: 0.56-0.59). S is SATURATES (0.20-0.30 / 0.54-0.57 / 0.38-0.64 / 0.41-0.50). F is ABSENT. So the expected reading is 'an algebraic RGI geometry exists at the natural delta, squeezing delta destroys it, inflating delta erodes it, and the model's own improvements do not land on it'.

 == limits
Scope:
- Weight space only. The MLP has no KV cache and no context state; see the KV measurement.
- The improvement is a single input column, similar to a soft token.
- 'Families' are widths, parameterisations and inits of one architecture, plus PC only if it trains (it fails level 1 at W=64 in calibration). There is no other architecture or tokenizer.

Method:
- Lambda and alpha use the anchors' shift labels, so they are properties of the geometry given the task semantics. The models never compose recipes or anchored edits themselves; only F measures what a model's own learning does.
- The late regulariser moves delta together with other things. Alpha expressivity falls to 0.83-0.98 at /4, and norms and saturation change. 'Causal for delta' therefore means 'causal for this intervention'; mediators are reported but cannot fully separate the effects.
- Free columns are carried only by linear anchor maps. Nonlinear or symmetry-aware maps are not tested.
- Alpha solutions are near-integer recipes that do not pool, so 'general' here means one-step portability, not accumulation. Recursion is lossy: about 0.7-0.8 per step.

Statistics:
- Noise is calibrated on 2 pilot worlds only.
- With 8 worlds, growth of 0.05 per doubling is found with probability 0.65, FALLS has low power (0.40), FLAT is reached only 65 % of the time, and slopes below about 0.03 per doubling cannot be resolved.

Interpretation:
- The pilot geometry (amplified random keys plus a periodic decoder, giving an additive algebra) comes from one-hot task slots entering the pre-activations additively. It says nothing directly about Qwen. The Pythia/Qwen delta is topic separation per block; the toy can only show whether such a separation is necessary, sufficient or harmful for portable improvements in a minimal system.
- The LLM counterparts would be task/function-vector arithmetic and anchor-relative coordinates (relative representations, Moschella et al. 2023).
- The 'ring structure' asked about is absent. Ring measures are kept as descriptive readings only; the shared structure that matters is algebraic, not metric.
== crit
 == matches_user_intent
Partly. The user asks whether a growing delta space produces a geometry that forces recursive general improvement (RGI). Under RGI, an improvement is not tied to the self: it extends to the model's own KV cache and to smaller, larger and other-family models, so RSI cannot stay local. The user wants this as more than 'non-local RSI that cannot be explained causally and locally'.

The Qwen design measures something real and close to this:
- whether the representation of 'improving X' is one direction for all X (target generality);
- whether self-improvement is absorbed into that direction (the geometric form of 'does RSI vanish with delta');
- whether the existing RSI loop drifts toward text about general improvement.

It does not measure an ability, and it says so. Its main gaps:
- 'Forces' is only asserted. Nothing in M1-M6 is causal. Its linear argument that no self direction could be pushed without also pushing g is false whenever TG < 1, because the self contrast minus its projection on g is such a push.
- Its 9 categories do not encode the user's two factors: whose (own / smaller / larger / other family) and what (weights / KV cache / learning).
- The decisive control is missing: neutral editing ('changes X'). Without it, 'more general than describe/use' is expected from verb semantics alone.
- Its second verb family is target-heterogeneous ('copies its own weights' means self-replication).
- Sentence lengths are not matched.
- Delta cannot be separated from depth within one model.

The toy is closer to the user's meaning. It asks whether an improvement made in one model works in a smaller, larger or differently trained model. It also separates three levels: improvements the geometry dictates (zero-shot recipe), permits (carried alpha) and forces (the model's own improvement lands on the shared form). But it has three weaknesses:
- The carrier is the experimenter (labels, ridge maps), not a model.
- All 'families' share the exact task group. My checks show that carried improvements survive partial task overlap, a different task family and an extra layer, so the transfer is a ceiling, not a test.
- The delta probe has no sham control. An off-target regulariser of the same size erodes Lambda and S as much as 'inflating delta' does. Lowering delta by that route barely hurts them. Only pulling the task clouds together collapses them. So the predicted 'optimum at natural delta' is a perturbation effect, and the scalar delta is not the operative variable.

The revision keeps both parts:
- Qwen: retargeted to an owner x substrate factorial with a change control and matched lengths, plus a cheap causal (gradient) forcing readout.
- Toy: sham-controlled delta arms, an early-regulariser arm, harder family variants, and a two-step rule. The rule calls a delta effect only when the effect does not depend on how delta was moved.

PART B: TOY

B1 Lines
- Lambda: zero-shot recipe accuracy, c_a + c_b - c_c over all anchor triples with a + b - c = c_new. Nulls: wrong-shift triples and permuted labels.
- S: alpha learned in A (12 coordinates, 8 shots, 300 steps), carried into B of another init at the same condition. Gain over 30 permuted correspondences. X = acc_A(alpha) / acc_A(free).

- Recursion: recipe-depth retention, round trip, pooling, and warm start (new). Warm start: B refines from the carried alpha; report accuracy after 10 and 40 steps minus the same from zero.
- T4: A's outputs on unlabeled inputs train B's column. This is the functional route and the only channel where A itself acts on B.

- Natural: checkpoint at first95 + 500, plus first95 + 1500 for within-run co-variation.
- Task regulariser, late (GLATE): R0-calibrated levels for x = log2(delta/delta_nat) of about -2, -1, +1, +2. Pilot values: about +0.01, +0.003, -0.004, -0.02.
- Sham regulariser, late: gamma * log F_x(h2), the Fisher ratio of h2 clouds grouped by the x input.
  - Levels: +0.01, -0.01, -0.03, -0.1.
  - Same parameter displacement as the task levels; moves task delta from x1.45 down to x0.2.
- Early arm, from step 1:
  - task: +0.003, -0.003, -0.01
  - sham: +0.003, -0.003, -0.01
  - +0.01 fails level 1 in both.
- Covariates per model:
  - x = log2(delta / delta_nat)
  - p = relative parameter distance to the same-init natural endpoint (late arm), or |gamma| / 0.01 (early arm)
  - k = 1 for the task regulariser, 0 otherwise
- Validity per world: every included model passes level 1, and the late task arm spans at least x8 in delta. At least 6 valid worlds are needed.

B3 Family arm (natural delta)
- B variants:
  - other init
  - other split
  - permuted x/y coding (an isomorphism, used as a control)
  - 8 of 12 shifts shared, plus the spare shift; alpha learned over the shared anchors
  - task family (x + 2y + c) mod 17
  - 3 hidden layers
  - ReLU, only if it passes level 1 after its own lr calibration (it fails at 1e-3, 3e-4 and 1e-4 now)
  - widths 64-1024, BP-SP, and PC if it passes G1-G5 (the original cross-model matrix)
- Lines: S, Lambda_B, F, T4.

B4 Statistics and rule (frozen before R1)
- Lines: Lambda, S-gain, free T1 gain, Phi.
- Models fitted per world and line by OLS on the model values:
  - full: y = a + b- x- + b+ x+ + e p + k (c0 + d- x- + d+ x+), with x- = min(x, 0) and x+ = max(x, 0)
  - reduced: the same without the k terms
- The late arm is primary, the early arm secondary. Coefficients are averaged over the valid worlds with a t 95 % interval.
- Step 1: the verdict is 'regulariser-specific (not delta)' if the interval of d- or d+ excludes 0 and |mean| >= 0.05 per doubling.
- Step 2, otherwise, from the reduced model:
  - GROWS beyond natural: b+ lower bound > 0 and mean >= 0.05 per doubling
  - ERODES: b+ upper bound < 0 and mean <= -0.05
  - FLAT beyond: b+ interval inside +-0.05
  - NEEDS delta: b- lower bound > 0 and mean >= 0.05
  - Label 'perturbation-driven' if the upper bound of e < 0.
- ABSENT: every condition's mean has upper bound < 0.05.
- Machinery gate: Lambda must be present at natural delta.
- Simulated behaviour (crit/sim_reg2.py; pilot noise, 8 worlds x 3 inits, 1,500 runs):

  | True scenario | Verdict and rate |
  |---|---|
  | Perturbation only | FLAT beyond 0.89 (false GROWS <= 0.01, false regulariser-specific 0.08) |
  | Pilot-like collapse under the task squeeze | regulariser-specific 1.00 |
  | Growth 0.10 per doubling | GROWS 0.92 (0.95 with an extra x2 sham arm) |
  | Growth 0.05 per doubling | GROWS 0.43-0.47 (cannot be resolved) |
  | Needs 0.10 per doubling below natural | NEEDS 0.92 |
  | Erodes above + needs below | 0.89-0.92 |

  The one-step additive regression gave a false GROWS in 100 % of pilot-like runs (crit/sim_reg.py).
- Natural co-variation (pre-registered, descriptive): the change of delta and of each line within a run, from +500 to +1500 updates, and the correlation across inits.
- Family verdict: 'portable across these families' if the S-gain lower bound > 0.10 in every variant that trains. Reported as an upper bound for real model families.
- Answer mapping. Lambda and S must agree for any delta claim; there is no OR across lines.
  - 'Delta builds a geometry that forces RGI': F GROWS, and Lambda and S GROW.
  - 'Delta permits RGI but does not force it': Lambda and S GROW, and F is ABSENT or FLAT.
  - 'RGI needs task separation up to the natural level; more adds nothing': NEEDS plus FLAT beyond, for Lambda and S.
  - 'More delta erodes it': ERODES for both.
  - 'Not delta: the shape of the space matters, not its size': regulariser-specific, or perturbation-driven with flat b+ and b-, for both.
  - 'No RGI geometry': all lines ABSENT.
  - Otherwise UNDECIDED. Recursion labels as designed.
- Expectation stated now, from the critique checks on 2 worlds:
  - Lambda and S: 'regulariser-specific (not delta)' and perturbation-driven.
  - F: ABSENT.
  - Family arm: portable (S-gain +0.37 to +0.72).
  - In the user's terms: a portable, recipe-like improvement format exists at every delta that training produces, and it carries across sizes and families. The model's own improvements do not take that form, and enlarging the semantic space neither builds nor forces it. Only pulling the task clouds together destroys it.
